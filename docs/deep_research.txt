2.3 What's specifically wrong with the current indicator set

Concrete observations:
    Fixed 3% stop-loss for everything (stoploss = -0.03). A 3% stop on BTC (daily ATR ~2%) is
    reasonable. A 3% stop on a meme coin (daily ATR ~10%) is noise -- you'll get stopped out on
    normal wiggle. ATR-based stops would help materially [opinion].
    EMA(12)/EMA(50) on 5m is sensitive to noise. Whipsaw is constant. Most pro setups use
    EMA(20)/EMA(50) or EMA(21)/EMA(55) [opinion].
    The "1h informative" timeframe is the only multi-timeframe context. No daily or weekly trend.
    The BTC regime gate is the first piece of cross-pair context the strategy has, added today.
    No price-relative-to-level features. "How far from yesterday's high?" or "is current price above the
    prior weekly close?" -- simple, free, and absent.

2.4 The "rule-based vs ML" question

The bot is 100% rule-based. CryptoBERT scores sentiment but does not predict price. [opinion]: ML for
crypto price prediction is hard and overrated -- most retail-built ML bots fail because the signal-to-noise
ratio is low and the data quality bar is high. The features the strategy already computes are exactly the
kind of inputs that would feed a lightweight model (e.g. LightGBM). But you should not put ML on top of
an unproven strategy -- first prove the rules work, then add ML to extract more juice. Doing it the other
way is "we don't know which inputs matter, let's hope the algorithm figures it out", and it doesn't.

PART 3 -- Strategy quality

3.1 What kind of strategy is this?

After v2 changes:
· Family: Momentum-pullback ("buy the dip after a breakout")
· Timeframe: 5m trades, average hold 18 minutes in backtest
· Edge hypothesis: Coins that pump and then briefly retrace tend to continue if the pullback is shallow
· Regime gate: Don't trade in a BTC downtrend (medium-term)

This is a reasonable, well-understood strategy family. Many professional discretionary traders use it. It's
not exotic, which is good -- exotic strategies typically don't survive after fees.

3.3 The "is this the best strategy?" question, honestly

[opinion] Short answer: it's a reasonable strategy family but currently a fragile prototype.
Strengths:
· Multi-source feature set (price + sentiment + on-chain + regime)
· Pullback entry is statistically more robust than chasing breakouts
· Regime gate prevents bear-market disasters
· Position-sizing discipline (fixed $500 / 3 max)

Weaknesses:
· Trade frequency too low -- 5 trades / 30 days won't compound
· Win:loss ratio less than 1 -- avg winner is smaller than avg loser
· One-size-fits-all stop-loss -- 3% on every coin regardless of volatility
· Strict path is dead -- 0 trades fired over a month
· No daily/weekly context -- operates entirely on 5m + 1h
· Single direction -- long-only, can't profit from down-moves

3.4 Alternative strategy families worth knowing about

[opinion] -- retail crypto strategy taxonomy:
1. Trend-following on higher timeframes (1h­4h) -- buy higher highs in confirmed uptrend, exit on

    trend break. Long holds. Simple. Robust. Few-times-a-month with big winners.
2. Mean reversion in range markets -- buy oversold (RSI<20, lower Bollinger), sell overbought.

    Works in chop, blows up in trending moves. Needs regime filter.
3. Pullback in trend (this bot) -- buy dips in confirmed uptrend. Medium frequency.
4. Breakout -- buy when price crosses a multi-day high. High false-positive rate; needs strong filters.
5. Cross-exchange arbitrage -- capture price differences between Binance and other venues.
    Capital-intensive but low-risk.

6. Funding rate arbitrage (futures-only) -- short the perp when funding is extreme positive, hold spot.
    Market-neutral. Real edge but small per trade.

7. Event/news trading -- fire on specific catalysts. The catalyst-pairlist work is the foundation for this.

The current pullback strategy is in family #3. The closest pivot that might add edge without changing the
family is to also look for breakouts of consolidation ranges (family #4) when the regime is bullish.

PART 4 -- Futures and shorts: the honest
economic analysis

4.1 Cost structure differences (current Binance rates)

Cost          Binance Spot          Binance USDs Futures

Maker fee     0.1% (0.075% w/ BNB)  0.02% (0.018% w/ BNB)
Taker fee     0.1% (0.075% w/ BNB)  0.04% (0.036% w/ BNB)

Funding rate  N/A                   Paid every 8h, range: typically
                                    -0.075% to +0.1% per period
Borrow cost   N/A
                                    Built into funding

Futures fees are actually lower than spot. The friction is the funding rate, paid every 8 hours by longs
(when positive) or shorts (when negative). In a strong bull market, longs can pay 0.05­0.1% per 8h =
0.15­0.3% per day just to hold. For a strategy holding 18 minutes on average, funding is irrelevant --
you never see a funding event.

4.2 The killer math for the current strategy on futures

Round-trip cost of one trade:
· Spot taker: 0.075% × 2 = 0.15% per trade
· Futures taker: 0.036% × 2 = 0.072% per trade
· Plus typical slippage: ~0.05% [opinion]

Total round-trip friction:
· Spot: ~0.20%
· Futures: ~0.12%

Current strategy's average winning trade: ~1.04%. Average losing trade: ~-2.02%. Net per trade:

4.3 Liquidation risk at common leverage levels

Approximation (ignoring small maintenance-margin adjustments):
· 1× leverage: no liquidation risk (same as spot)
· 2× leverage: liquidated at ~50% adverse move
· 3× leverage: liquidated at ~33% adverse move
· 5× leverage: liquidated at ~20% adverse move
· 10× leverage: liquidated at ~10% adverse move
· 20× leverage: liquidated at ~5% adverse move
[opinion] Crypto sees 10­20% single-day moves routinely. Luna: -99% in 3 days. FTX: -90% in 48h.
Anything above 3× leverage on a single position is gambling, not trading. For a momentum strategy with
3% hard stops, 2­3× leverage is the practical max -- and even that requires the stop-loss to never slip
during a fast move.

4.4 Do shorts work in crypto historically?

[opinion]:
· Bull markets: shorts are graveyards. Coins that "look done" can melt up another 100%.
· Bear markets: shorts work, but the easy short trades happen in the first 30% of the drawdown -- by

    the time most people recognize "we're in a bear", the easy money is gone.
· Ranging markets: short the resistance, long the support. Mean-reversion shorts. Bot-friendly.
· Single-event shorts: token unlocks, security exploits, exchange depegs. High edge if you're early;

    impossible to scale because they're rare.
· Funding-rate shorts: when perp funding is extreme positive, short and earn the funding while you're

    correct on direction. Real market-neutral edge.

The bot's current regime gate gives a partial answer to "long or short": when BTC EMA48 < EMA200,
don't long. The mirror would be: when BTC EMA48 < EMA200, consider shorting bounces in weak
coins.

4.5 Is shorting worth it for this bot, today?

Direct answer: not yet, and putting the current strategy on futures of any flavor will lose money.
Three conditions need to be met first:

    The long-side strategy needs to clear round-trip fees on average. It doesn't. Win:loss ratio
    improvement is required. Either tighter stops, partial-profit-taking, or wider thresholds for catching
    bigger moves.
    The bot needs a separate short-trigger signal distinct from the long trigger. The mirror image of
    "pullback after pump" isn't "bounce after dump" -- bounces after dumps usually succeed in crypto.
    The real short signal is "failed bounce after dump" -- price tries to recover, fails to clear resistance,
    rolls over. Different pattern.
    Sample size on shorts needs to be backtestable. That means at least one bear or distribution
    market in the backtest window. Our 30-day window has BTC up 26% -- there is no shortable regime,
    so we cannot validate shorts on this data.

To get from here to "futures with shorts" responsibly:
· Tune the long side so it clears futures friction. Bar: avg win  0.5%, win rate  55%, profit factor  1.4.
· Wait for or download a bear-market window in the historical data.
· Implement short logic (mirror image with the right pattern) and backtest it on that bear window.
· Only then flip the live config to futures, dry-run for 2 weeks, and finally consider live.

This sequence is the difference between "futures bot that loses money quickly" and "futures bot that
captures both directions of crypto's volatility."

TL;DR -- direct answers

Is the bot getting the best information?
No. It gets good, free, broad information, but with clear gaps: (a) three valuable signals (LSR ratios,
taker buy/sell ratio) collected and not used in the strategy; (b) missing order-book depth and liquidations,
both free and high-value; (c) missing common indicators like ATR and Bollinger.
Is the strategy the best?
Reasonable family (pullback-in-trend), but currently a thin prototype: 5 trades / 30 days, +0.05% per
trade, likely below round-trip fees in real conditions. Structural fixes (ATR stops, ROI ladder tuning,
more triggers) are clear next steps.
Is futures + shorts worth it?
Not yet. Current edge is below the fee floor on spot already, futures funding cost would push it deeper
into the red, and shorts can't be validated against our current (all-bull-market) backtest data. Honest
sequence: (1) tune longs to clearly clear fees, (2) acquire bear-market historical data, (3) write and
backtest short logic, (4) switch live to futures dry-run, (5) only then real money on shorts. Anything faster
is gambling, not trading.
